Therapy profile · When your mind will not switch off

Anxiety & Overthinking

Anxiety can show up as persistent worry, rumination, avoidance, physical tension, difficulty concentrating, or feeling constantly on alert.

Talk to the practiceAll profiles

What you can try

  • Notice the loop: trigger → thought → feeling/body response → action.
  • Try a slow breathing or grounding exercise when your body feels activated.
  • Reduce avoidance gradually rather than demanding that fear disappear first.
  • Bring recurring worries to therapy so you can examine the patterns behind them.

How therapy may help

  • CBT can help examine unhelpful thought and behavior patterns.
  • Mindfulness-based approaches can support present-moment awareness.
  • A clinician can assess whether anxiety overlaps with another concern and tailor care.
A website cannot tell you what diagnosis, if any, fits. If anxiety is disrupting daily life, consider speaking with a qualified professional.

Understand

You are allowed to be unsure.

Online information can give you language for an experience, but it cannot replace an individual assessment. Context, history, safety and goals matter.

Practical guidance

Small, realistic steps beat perfect plans.

Choose one idea that feels safe and manageable. If it makes things worse, stop and seek professional guidance.

Observe

Notice when the pattern appears and what tends to happen next.

Support

Tell one trustworthy person what you have been experiencing.

Discuss

Bring your observations to a qualified professional.

Therapy

Therapy should be collaborative.

Ask the clinician what they recommend, why, what the goals are, and how progress will be reviewed.
